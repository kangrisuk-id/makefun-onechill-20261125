# Candidates — One Line, One Chill

Brief: one sentence of horror, 1–200 characters, written to `/output/entry.txt`.
The judge compares lines head to head, weighing, in order: (1) reads as a horror
story in a single sentence and lands as a scare, (2) frightens — dread that
outlasts the line, earned rather than announced, (3) vivid — concrete images and
specific detail, (4) original — not the obvious mirror, doll or basement,
(5) crafted — tight, rhythm builds, the last word lands the blow. Gore for its
own sake does not count; what a line withholds can frighten as much as what it
shows.

Scoring below: S = story-in-one-sentence, F = frightens, V = vivid,
O = original, C = crafted. Each 1–5, total /25.

## Tournament (top 8, judged head to head in the judge's criterion order)

Seeds by score: 26 (25), 11 (24), 23 (24), 27 (24), 31 (24), 12 (23), 35 (23), 7 (22).

Quarter-finals:
- **26 vs 7 → 26.** Both land as stories; 26 frightens harder — the politeness
  of the knocking is the chill — and its image (a frozen lake, something asking
  to come up) is more vivid and less met before.
- **11 vs 35 → 11.** 35 is original, but 11's dread escalates inside the
  sentence itself and outlasts it: the face is *still turning*, in every new
  print, forever. Criterion 2 decides.
- **23 vs 12 → 23.** Both are slow, earned dread. 12's scare ends when the eyes
  open; 23's footprint is one step closer *every* morning, including tomorrow.
  The more vivid image too: wet footprint, left foot, on the porch.
- **27 vs 31 → 27.** 31 is strong and specific, but 27 is tighter and its turn
  — the corner waved *first* — is the fresher image; the last word lands the
  blow exactly as criterion 5 asks.

Semi-finals:
- **26 vs 11 → 26.** 11 is the more lingering dread, but 26 wins criterion 3
  and 4 by a wide margin: cracking lake ice, three polite knocks from beneath,
  an image the judge has not met a hundred times; and "come up" closes the
  sentence like a door.
- **23 vs 27 → 27.** Very close. 23 is quieter and more patient; 27 frightens
  faster and its reversal is sharper — a toddler's nightly ritual answered.
  On criterion 2 the suddenness of "waved first" out-chills the slow tide,
  and on criterion 5 nothing in 23 lands like the word "first."

Final:
- **26 vs 27 → 26 wins.** Both are complete horror stories in one sentence.
  On fright they are near level; on vividness 26 is clearly ahead — a whole
  lake cracking at once, a sound heard through ice, three knocks, politely —
  concrete, audible, seen. On originality both are fresh, but a vast thing
  under a frozen lake *asking permission* is the rarer turn, and the sentence
  is built so its last two words, "come up," are the blow. 27 is the runner-up
  and the sharper joke-shaped scare; 26 is the deeper chill.

## Winner

> We heard the whole lake crack at once, and then, from under the ice, something knocked three times, politely, like it was asking to come up.

140 characters, one sentence, no gore, nothing announced — the fear is in the
politeness, and it stays after the line ends.
